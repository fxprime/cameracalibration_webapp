03 / WHY MANY VIEWS?

Give the solver evidence

A front-facing board at one distance does not constrain everything. Tilt it around two axes, vary distance, and cover the edges.

Fit at least 10 views; aim for 20–40 sharp, diverse views plus separate validation images. Keep focus, resolution, crop and lens fixed.

What “good” means for VIO and 3D scanning

Start with a practical review threshold of 0.5 px RMS; this is a lab target, not a universal certification. Inspect residuals and held-out views, coverage, pose diversity, parameter stability and the undistorted image. A low training error can still hide a bent board or a wrong lens model.

OpenVINS recommends careful camera and camera–IMU calibration. ORB-SLAM3 supports pinhole and fisheye cameras. Intrinsics are only one part: VIO also needs camera–IMU geometry and timing; multi-camera scanning needs camera extrinsics. This lab does single-camera intrinsics and tag pose.

Global shutter still blurs during exposure. Keep the board still, add light, lock short exposure when the camera permits, and avoid extreme gain. Sharpening cannot recover accurate corners lost to motion blur.

Every corner is compared with its model projection. Reserve new views to check whether the fitted geometry generalizes.

Read the real VINS-Mono camera interface

The tracker lifts each observed pixel through its calibrated camera model into a 3D bearing. A bearing supplies a direction; it does not supply scene depth. Projection maps the bearing back to the image to check the model.

Kannala–Brandt: the names and coefficient order

In this VINS-Mono revision, the YAML model is KANNALA_BRANDT and the C++ class is EquidistantCamera. Its radial law is an odd polynomial in ray angle. The zero-coefficient case is ideal equidistant projection; the coefficients perturb that law.

The symmetric four-coefficient angular polynomial corresponds to this lab’s OpenCV fisheye / Kalibr equidistant model under the same coordinate and skew assumptions. The broader Kannala–Brandt family can have additional terms: the name alone is not enough to identify a compatible model. Pinhole radtan coefficients are powers of normalized-plane radius, so they cannot be renamed into angular KB coefficients.
