What are we finding?

Calibration estimates the camera’s internal geometry. With it, a pixel becomes a direction in 3D, and a known target can provide metric pose.

Before distortion: λ [u, v, 1]ᵀ = K [R | t] [X, Y, Z, 1]ᵀ

K: focal lengths fₓ, fᵧ and principal point cₓ, cᵧ.
D: lens distortion. R, t: target pose for each view.

K = ⎡ fₓ 0 cₓ ⎤
　　⎢ 0 fᵧ cᵧ ⎥
　　⎣ 0 0 1 ⎦

For a centered, undistorted pinhole: HFOV = 2 atan(width / 2fₓ). Real distorted FOV requires the fitted model. Sensor size and lens focal length in mm are different from fₓ in pixels.

01 / CAMERA MODEL

Pinhole + radtan

A sensible starting model for this module’s moderate field of view. Fit fₓ, fᵧ, cₓ, cᵧ and k₁, k₂, p₁, p₂. k₃ stays fixed to avoid needless complexity.

x = X/Z, y = Y/Z, r² = x²+y²
x′ = x(1+k₁r²+k₂r⁴)
　+ 2p₁xy + p₂(r²+2x²)
u = fₓx′ + cₓ

y′ uses the analogous tangential terms. Calibration minimizes pixel reprojection residuals across all views.

02 / WIDE ANGLE

Pinhole + equidistant

OpenCV’s fisheye model and Kalibr’s equidistant distortion use four angular coefficients. Choose it when the lens geometry warrants it; compare held-out performance.

θ = atan(√(x²+y²))
θd = θ(1+k₁θ²+k₂θ⁴+k₃θ⁶+k₄θ⁸)

Do not choose a model from a single FOV threshold or transplant coefficients between models.

03 / WHY MANY VIEWS?

Give the solver evidence

A front-facing board at one distance does not constrain everything. Tilt it around two axes, vary distance, and cover the edges.

Fit at least 10 views; aim for 20–40 sharp, diverse views plus separate validation images. Keep focus, resolution, crop and lens fixed.

What “good” means for VIO and 3D scanning

Start with a practical review threshold of 0.5 px RMS; this is a lab target, not a universal certification. Inspect residuals and held-out views, coverage, pose diversity, parameter stability and the undistorted image. A low training error can still hide a bent board or a wrong lens model.

OpenVINS recommends careful camera and camera–IMU calibration. ORB-SLAM3 supports pinhole and fisheye cameras. Intrinsics are only one part: VIO also needs camera–IMU geometry and timing; multi-camera scanning needs camera extrinsics. This lab does single-camera intrinsics and tag pose.

Global shutter still blurs during exposure. Keep the board still, add light, lock short exposure when the camera permits, and avoid extreme gain. Sharpening cannot recover accurate corners lost to motion blur.

RMS = √[ (1/N) Σᵢ ((uᵢ − ûᵢ)² + (vᵢ − v̂ᵢ)²) ]

Every corner is compared with its model projection. Reserve new views to check whether the fitted geometry generalizes.

These are actual example intrinsics from the project, not accuracy guarantees or values to copy to your camera. The small equidistant coefficients do not mean the TUM VI lens behaves like an undistorted pinhole: the angular projection is already part of its model.

For 3D reconstruction, Open3D RGB-D integration uses a pinhole intrinsic matrix alongside depth scale and camera poses. Correct lens distortion before a pipeline that expects rectified pinhole images, and supply the corresponding new K. Intrinsic calibration does not replace depth-camera alignment or depth-scale calibration.

Choose and measure your board

Print at 100% / actual size. Disable fit-to-page. A board larger than your paper requires larger paper; never silently shrink it. Measure the printed black tag edge or square size with a ruler and enter the measured size. Glue to a flat, rigid backing.

AprilGrid: coded IDs tolerate partial visibility and remove checkerboard orientation ambiguity. Checkerboard: cheap, precise subpixel corners, but all internal corners must be visible here. A 9×6 internal-corner board has 10×7 squares.

Keep a white border. Our AprilGrid uses IDs from zero, left-to-right starting at the bottom row, with tag36h11. Single-tag pose uses the measured black outer edge, excluding white margin.
